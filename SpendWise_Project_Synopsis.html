1. INTRODUCTION

Personal financial management has evolved rapidly with the growth of digital commerce. In contemporary urban economies, a single consumer can execute dozens of micro-transactions within hours across digital payment gateways, mobile wallets, point-of-sale terminals and automated subscription debits. This section introduces the background of the personal finance domain, provides an architectural overview of the proposed application, and explains the motivation and scope of the work.

1.1 Background

The rapid proliferation of digital payment instruments—notably the Unified Payments Interface (UPI), contactless debit/credit card terminals, and automated electronic clearing house mandates—has fundamentally altered consumer spending psychology. Historically, the tactile friction of counting and exchanging physical paper currency served as an intuitive psychological brake against impulsive consumption. In today's frictionless financial ecosystem, consumers frequently suffer from "spending blindness," a behavioral condition where cumulative discretionary micro-purchases exhaust liquid disposable income well before the end of the monthly billing cycle.

Most commercial expense trackers fail to resolve this issue effectively. Existing market solutions either require invasive synchronization with personal bank credentials (posing severe privacy, credential leakage, and identity theft risks), inundate users with predatory credit marketing, or present complex double-entry accounting spreadsheets that demand excessive manual upkeep. For students, freelancers, and early-career professionals operating with finite monthly resources, the lack of real-time, explainable, and proactive budgetary feedback leads to recurrent deficit cycles and sub-optimal savings habits.

At the same time, modern web engineering standards—such as declarative component-driven single-page applications, reactive client-side state caching, and lightweight relational persistence engines—make it possible to build responsive, privacy-preserving financial management platforms that operate without external corporate tracking. This enables the design of tools that track expenditures with mathematical precision, evaluate fiscal stability through an objective financial health metric, and alert users to budgetary breaches before liquidity is compromised.

1.2 Project Overview

SpendWise is a web-based, full-stack application developed using React 19 and Node.js. The user manages income and expenditure records through an intuitive, mobile-responsive interface. Transaction entries are tagged with categories, payment modes, and dates, and sent via authenticated REST requests to an Express.js server backed by Prisma ORM and SQLite. The backend aggregates transactions, checks spending against monthly category budgets, and calculates an objective Financial Health Score (0–100) alongside daily run-rates and month-end spending forecasts. The results are visualized through interactive charts and can be exported as reports.

The project is an applied full-stack engineering effort; it combines relational database modeling with responsive frontend architecture, heuristic analytical algorithms, and comprehensive data export capabilities.

1.3 Motivation and Scope

The motivation for this project comes from the experience of students and young professionals who find themselves running out of disposable funds midway through the month without understanding where their capital was spent. A tool that provides immediate, visual, and mathematically explainable feedback on personal spending can lower the barrier to disciplined budgeting and foster sustainable saving habits. The scope of the project is deliberately defined so that it can be completed and evaluated within the project period:

  • In scope: secure multi-user registration, password hashing with Bcrypt, and stateless session management with JWT over HTTP-Only cookies with Bearer fallback.
  • In scope: full CRUD transaction management with multi-criteria filtering (date ranges, types, categories, search), pagination, and atomic batch deletion.
  • In scope: category budget allocation with dynamic percentage tracking and real-time color-coded alert thresholds (Green < 70%, Amber 70%–90%, Red > 90%).
  • In scope: deterministic Financial Health Scoring algorithm (0–100), daily spending run-rate computation, and projected month-end burn rate forecasting.
  • In scope: multi-horizon reporting (1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, All Time) analyzing payment modes (UPI, Card, Cash, Net Banking) and day-of-the-week spending patterns.
  • In scope: pixel-perfect printable financial statement generation and RFC 4180-compliant CSV spreadsheet export.

  • In scope: multi-currency display formatting (INR ₹, USD $, EUR €, GBP £), persistent dark/light theme switching, and a 1-click evaluator demo data seeder.
  • Out of scope: automated screen-scraping or live Open Banking API integration with commercial bank servers, to prevent security, credential leakage and privacy liabilities.
  • Out of scope: real-time foreign exchange currency trading or automated cross-currency arbitrage calculation.
  • Out of scope: optical character recognition (OCR) for scanned paper receipts (planned for future phases).

The decision to exclude automated bank screen-scraping is a deliberate security feature. Third-party scraping mechanisms require users to expose their net-banking user IDs, passwords, and multi-factor SMS tokens to intermediary aggregators. Recent regulatory audits and cybersecurity incidents demonstrate that storing plain-text or reversibly encrypted banking credentials on third-party cloud servers creates catastrophic attack surfaces. By prioritizing a self-contained, user-verified ledger model, SpendWise ensures complete user data sovereignty.

The Financial Health Score and budget projections produced by the system are deterministic analytical indicators derived from transparent mathematical formulas; they are designed to guide personal spending discipline and do not constitute formal investment or legal fiduciary advice. The metric provides an explainable heuristic that encourages disciplined savings ratios and balanced cash flows without opaque algorithmic bias.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT

2.1 Core Problem

Job seekers, students, and early-career candidates engage in continuous digital commerce without maintaining a consolidated ledger of their daily expenses. Existing financial applications fail to provide a balanced solution: commercial platforms operate behind paywalls, harvest private transaction metadata for advertising, and suffer from rigid synchronization dependencies, while traditional spreadsheet software requires tedious manual data entry, lacks proactive alerts, and offers no mobile responsiveness.

The core problem addressed by this project is the design and development of a secure, full-stack web application that accepts user financial entries, enforces strict category budgets, and reliably computes explainable financial analytics. The system must produce stable and deterministic results, meaning that identical transaction sets yield the same health score and run-rate projections. Beyond raw transaction logging, it must break down expenditures by payment modes, identify weekend lifestyle inflation, alert the user before a budget is breached, and deliver everything in an intuitive web interface with printable reports.

2.3 Constraints and Assumptions

The system operates under concrete infrastructural and operational constraints that shaped its technical implementation:

  • Local-First Database Constraint: The system relies on an embedded SQLite relational database (dev.db). This guarantees zero external server dependencies, instant setup, and native ACID compliance. However, write operations are subject to SQLite's file-level locking, which limits massive enterprise concurrency (thousands of simultaneous writes per second) while perfectly serving personal, single-tenant, or household deployments.
  • Network & API Independence: The core analytical engine executes 100% locally on the Node.js server without making outbound calls to third-party AI APIs or banking servers. This guarantees uninterrupted availability, zero API quota errors, and absolute data privacy.
  • Input Validation & Boundary Assumptions: Transaction amounts are assumed to be non-zero positive numeric values. Dates are assumed to be stored in standard ISO 8601 compliant UTC format with client-side localization. Users are assumed to record transactions manually, import them via CSV, or utilize the built-in demo seeder.
  • Security & Transport Assumptions: In production deployments, the application assumes transport over HTTPS/TLS to safeguard session cookies against man-in-the-middle attacks. It assumes the host operating system enforces standard file permission boundaries on the SQLite database file.

These constraints ensure that SpendWise remains lightweight, easily auditable, and capable of operating offline or on low-cost single-board computers (such as Raspberry Pi home servers) without ongoing cloud hosting expenditure.

4. PROPOSED SOLUTION

This section describes how SpendWise solves the problem defined above: the shortcomings of existing approaches, the overall solution, its architecture and methodology, and the engineering techniques that make the full-stack system consistent and robust.

4.1 Limitations of Existing Systems

Automated personal financial tracking has evolved from physical paper ledgers, through manual spreadsheets, to commercial cloud applications. Spreadsheets require excessive manual maintenance and lack proactive alerts. Commercial apps such as Mint, YNAB, and PocketGuard offer budgeting tools, but they are typically subscription-based, demand bank credential linking, and monetize user data. Existing solutions generally suffer from the following limitations:

  • They focus on a single function, such as manual logging or expense splitting, rather than an integrated budgetary workflow.
  • Their health evaluation is opaque or non-existent, offering no objective mathematical score that a candidate can inspect.
  • Results are inconsistent across platforms, with few tools providing predictive month-end outlays or daily burn-rates.
  • They do not combine temporal habit tracking (e.g. weekend vs. weekday spending) with payment mode breakdown.
  • They offer limited data sovereignty, locking historical accounting data behind subscription paywalls or proprietary formats.

4.2 Overview of the Proposed Solution

The proposed system overcomes these limitations through an integrated, consistent and explainable pipeline, shown in Figure 4.1. The user enters financial records via the web UI; inputs are validated on the client and sent as authenticated REST payloads to an Express API server. Prisma ORM compiles type-safe SQL queries against an embedded SQLite database. Analytical controllers aggregate outlays, evaluate category budgets, and calculate the 0–100 Health Score before returning structured JSON responses. Results are visualized through reactive Recharts widgets or exported as reports.

  1. Relational Persistence: Prisma Client compiles parameterized SQL statements, inserting records into SQLite with foreign key cascades.

4.5 Algorithmic and Relational Design

To ensure consistent and reliable performance across the application lifecycle, three recurring design patterns are enforced:

  • Strict Parameterized Queries: All database queries are executed through Prisma ORM prepared statements, eliminating SQL injection risks and guaranteeing type safety. User input is treated strictly as literal data rather than executable SQL syntax, ensuring that malicious payload strings cannot manipulate query structure.
  • Relational Referential Integrity: SQLite foreign keys are configured with onDelete: Cascade for user-owned records (transactions, budgets) and onDelete: Restrict for categories with historical records. This ensures that deleting a user account cleanly purges all dependent rows without leaving orphaned ledger records, while preventing accidental category deletions that would corrupt historical accounting reports.
  • Deterministic Algorithmic Bounds: The Financial Health Score is mathematically clamped between 10 and 98 to eliminate erratic swings from single transactions, ensuring a stable, explainable metric across varying income scales.

4.6 Financial Health Scoring Rubric and Deterministic Generation

The largest source of inconsistency in early expense trackers was subjective grading. It was replaced by an additive mathematical point allocation, shown in Figure 4.4 and Table 4.2. Each category is evaluated independently, so the final score is fully explainable.

Figure 4.4: Point allocation of the Financial Health scoring rubric (maximum 100)
Table 4.2: Financial Health scoring rubric
Scoring Category Condition Points
Baseline Score System neutral starting value 50
Savings Rate (Rs) ≥ 50% (+25) | ≥ 30% (+18) | ≥ 15% (+10) | ≤ 0% (-15) +25 to -15
Budget Discipline 0 breaches (+15) | Exceeded: -(N × 8) +15 to -24
Cash Flow Polarity Total Income > Expense (+10) | Expense > Income (-15) +10 to -15
Clamping Bounds Score = max(10, min(98, round(RawScore))) 10 to 98

The rubric is combined with deterministic evaluation logic. The qualitative tiers are mapped to: 85–98 (Excellent), 70–84 (Very Good), 50–69 (Moderate), and < 50 (Needs Improvement). This gives candidates clear, actionable feedback: a user who earns a moderate income but maintains a 40% savings rate and zero budget overruns achieves an Excellent rating, whereas a high earner who consistently exceeds category budgets is alerted to fiscal distress.

4.7 API Call Handling, Middleware and Error Logic

All client requests pass through an Express middleware chain. If the JWT token is missing or invalid, an HTTP 401 response is returned before route controllers execute. Resource mutations verify ownership against the active user ID, returning HTTP 403 upon mismatch (Figure 4.5).

12

The API pipeline is designed for fault tolerance and predictability. When an authenticated client dispatches a mutation request, Express executes the authorization check first. If valid, the request payload is parsed and validated against expected data types. If a required field is missing (such as omitting a transaction amount or category ID), the API returns a structured HTTP 400 Bad Request error. If a user attempts to modify or delete a transaction belonging to another account, the query filter catches the ownership mismatch and emits an HTTP 403 Forbidden status, preventing unauthorized lateral privilege escalation.

4.8 State Management

In client-side single-page applications, UI state must remain synchronized across page changes and background fetches. SpendWise encapsulates auth credentials, currency selection, and theme preferences within React Context providers (AuthContext, CurrencyContext, ThemeContext). When a transaction is added or deleted, a global event triggers instant re-fetching across dashboard analytics components (Figure 4.6).

13
Figure 4.6: Client synchronization cycle and use of event state

The state management lifecycle in SpendWise balances reactivity with network conservation. In traditional architectures, component-level state changes frequently trigger cascade re-renders across the entire virtual DOM tree, or require complex Redux middleware. SpendWise bypasses this overhead by pairing modular React Contexts with an asynchronous window event bus (window.dispatchEvent). When a mutation finishes, the event bus notifies interested consumers (such as the executive summary cards, Recharts visualizations, and budget alerts) to execute lightweight background queries.

This event-driven reactive cycle guarantees that KPI cards (Net Balance, Savings Rate, Month-End Projections) and charts update immediately whenever ledger mutations occur, preserving client bandwidth while maintaining visual consistency. Simultaneously, user visual preferences (Dark vs. Light mode) and active currency selections are persisted in browser localStorage, guaranteeing seamless session resumption on subsequent visits.

5.2 User Authentication and Session Management

The authentication module registers new users with Bcrypt password hashing (10 salt rounds) and issues signed JSON Web Tokens (JWT) with 7-day expiration. Tokens are transmitted within HTTP-Only cookies with sameSite: 'lax' to protect against XSS credential theft, with Authorization Bearer header fallback for external clients. Profile settings enable updating user names, default currencies, and overall target monthly caps.

5.3 Transaction Ledger Ingestion and Batch Management

The ledger supports full CRUD operations on income and expense transactions. The listing route supports multi-criteria query parameters (startDate, endDate, category, type, search) with server-side pagination (skip, take). An atomic batch deletion endpoint (POST /batch-delete) allows multi-row removal while validating user ownership. A global keyboard shortcut (pressing 'N') opens the modal anywhere in the app.

5.4 Dynamic Budget Allocation and Variance Tracking

Users establish monthly spending limits per category for given YYYY-MM periods. The server computes actual outlays via prisma.transaction.aggregate() and triages consumption into Green (< 70%), Amber (70%–90%), and Red (> 90%) progress bands, with warning badges alerting users before a budget is breached.

5.5 Financial Health and Predictive Run-Rate Engine

Implements the deterministic 3-factor algorithm described in Section 4.6 to produce an objective 0–100 score. The engine computes average daily outlays and estimates projected month-end expenditures based on elapsed calendar days, providing immediate feedback on whether monthly spending pace is sustainable.

5.6 Deep Analytical Reporting Across Time Horizons

Aggregates financial records across selectable time horizons (1 Month, 3 Months, 6 Months, 1 Year, All Time). A rolling 6-month grouped bar chart displays cash inflows against outflows, giving users clear visibility into liquidity trends, savings margins, and seasonal expenditures.

5.7 Payment Mode and Day-of-the-Week Habit Analysis

Categorizes expenditures across payment instruments (UPI, Credit Card, Cash, Net Banking) in a Recharts Donut chart. A day-of-the-week bar chart aggregates spending across Sunday through Saturday to identify weekend lifestyle inflation.

The interface uses a navy sidebar for navigation and live status metrics, and a clean main area with card-style content. The Financial Health Score and budget limits are rendered on visual progress gauges with three distinct color bands, illustrated in Figure 5.2. Theme persistence ensures seamless readability in both light and dark modes, conforming to WCAG 2.1 contrast standards.

5.9 Printable Financial Statement and CSV Export

The reports page features a print statement button that triggers window.print(), using specialized CSS @media print rules to format records into clean, printable physical or PDF statements without web navigation artifacts.

Evaluators can use the built-in 1-click seeder in Settings to populate 25 sample transactions and 5 category budgets instantly across a 3-month timeline, verifying charts and alerts without manual data entry. A corresponding reset button clears the user account back to a clean slate anytime.

7.3 Limitations

While SpendWise delivers an efficient, private personal accounting environment, several operational limitations exist:

  • The Financial Health Score is an automated heuristic estimate derived from mathematical formulas; it does not replace certified professional financial planning or tax advisory services.
  • Direct bank credential synchronization is excluded to protect user security and eliminate third-party dependencies, requiring users to log expenses manually or import them via CSV.
  • SQLite database write concurrency is constrained by file-level locking, making it suitable for single-user or small-group instances rather than massive multi-tenant enterprise clusters.
  • Currency conversion dynamically updates display symbols and localized formatting, but does not perform live foreign exchange rate calculations.

Overall, the project demonstrates that modern web standards and lightweight relational persistence engines provide a secure, responsive, and privacy-preserving platform for personal financial tracking.

8. FUTURE SCOPE

The modular architecture of SpendWise allows several extensions to be added without changing the core architectural pipeline:

  • Receipt Scanning with Google Gemini Vision API: Integration of multimodal vision models to extract merchant names, total amounts, dates, and suggested categories from uploaded receipt photos, eliminating manual keying.
  • Automated Bank SMS Notification Parsing: Development of a companion Android background service to parse bank transaction SMS notifications and stage draft transactions for one-tap user confirmation.
  • Predictive Machine Learning Anomaly Detection: Integration of local time-series forecasting models (e.g. ARIMA or Holt-Winters) to detect unusual spending spikes and forecast end-of-month cash balances based on seasonal patterns.
  • Progressive Web App (PWA) & Web Push Notifications: Service worker configuration to enable offline transaction logging and trigger browser push notifications when category budgets exceed 90%.
  • Collaborative Multi-User Household Budgets: Extension of the relational schema to support multi-user shared budgets with role-based permissions (admin, contributor, viewer) for families and shared apartments.
  • Automated Bank Statement PDF Ingestion: Implementation of an automated PDF parser to extract and classify hundreds of transactions directly from monthly bank statements.

In conclusion, the project offers a practical, technology-driven solution that improves financial literacy and budget discipline, and each of these extensions can build upon the existing architecture.
